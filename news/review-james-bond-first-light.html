007 First Light Review: IO Interactive Finally Makes You Feel Like James Bond

IO Interactive finally delivers the Bond fantasy we've been waiting for.

REVIEW • THE GAME TIMES

I came for James Bond. I stayed because IO Interactive somehow made me feel cooler than I actually am.

The Verdict

I'll be honest.

I was nervous about 007 First Light.

Not because it was made by IO Interactive.

Because it was made by IO Interactive.

The studio behind Hitman had to answer one ridiculous question:

How do you make a Bond game without simply making Hitman in a tuxedo?

Somehow...

they fucking did it.

007 First Light is not Hitman. It is not Uncharted. It is not a movie tie-in pretending to be a game.

It is an original James Bond origin story that understands something incredibly important:

Bond is not just about shooting people.

He is about improvisation. Bluffing. Stealth. Gadgets. Charm. Cars. Luxury. Violence. And occasionally doing something so unbelievably stupid that you wonder how MI6 has not fired him yet.

IO Interactive understands all of it.

And that is why 007 First Light is one of my favorite games of 2026.

This Isn't the Bond You Already Know

The smartest decision IO Interactive made was not starting with the finished James Bond.

This is Bond before the legend.

He is young.

Reckless.

Arrogant.

Impulsive.

And occasionally an absolute pain in the ass.

Patrick Gibson is fantastic in the role. He does not attempt to impersonate Daniel Craig, copy Pierce Brosnan, or imitate Roger Moore.

He creates his own Bond.

You can feel the confidence forming underneath the inexperience.

And that is what makes him interesting.

You are not playing as the world's greatest spy.

You are watching him become one.

That gives the story somewhere meaningful to go.

The Spy Fantasy Actually Works

This is where First Light surprised me.

Most games let you shoot like a spy.

This one lets you think like one.

IO Interactive built the experience around four major pillars: spycraft, instinct, gadgets, and combat.

And they are not just marketing words.

Spycraft allows Bond to observe environments, gather information and identify opportunities.

Instinct lets him bluff, deceive and adapt.

Gadgets provide different ways to approach situations.

And combat is what happens when your brilliant plan inevitably goes to shit.

Which...

it will.

That freedom is where First Light feels most like an authentic Bond experience.

When the Plan Goes to Shit

This might actually be my favorite part of the game.

You sneak into a location.

Everything is going perfectly.

You identify your target.

You quietly move past security.

Then someone spots you.

Fuck.

Now you are improvising.

Maybe you fight.

Maybe you run.

Maybe you use a gadget.

Maybe you bluff your way out.

Maybe you grab whatever weapon happens to be nearby.

Suddenly, you are doing something completely different from what you planned.

That is where IO's Hitman DNA becomes an advantage rather than a problem.

That's Bond.

The plan rarely survives contact with reality.

The Combat

I'm not going to pretend the shooting is the second coming of Max Payne 3.

It isn't.

But it does not need to be.

The combat is designed around movement and improvisation. Ammunition can be limited, weapons are not always permanent fixtures, melee is surprisingly important, environmental objects can become weapons, and Bond can transition between shooting, melee and gadgets quickly.

You are encouraged to keep moving rather than sitting behind cover and emptying magazines.

And some of the melee animations?

Absolutely filthy.

IO Interactive knows how to make a takedown look satisfying.

The Melee

This deserves its own section because I was not expecting it to be this good.

Bond does not fight like a superhero.

He fights like someone who really does not want to die.

Punches.

Throws.

Environmental takedowns.

Improvised attacks.

Everything has weight.

And because ammunition is not unlimited, getting close is not simply a cinematic option.

Sometimes...

it's necessary.

One minute you're Bond.

The next, you are throwing some poor bastard into a table because you ran out of bullets.

007, ladies and gentlemen.

The Hitman DNA — And Why It Doesn't Ruin Bond

Yes.

You can feel Hitman everywhere.

The environments.

The stealth.

The opportunities.

The improvisation.

The way IO Interactive designs spaces.

But here is the important difference:

Bond isn't Agent 47.

47 wants to disappear.

Bond wants to walk into the room wearing a tuxedo and somehow make everyone notice him.

First Light understands that distinction.

It is less about being the perfect invisible assassin and more about being the world's most resourceful idiot with a license to kill.

And that is much more fun.

A Proper World Tour

A Bond game needs to make you feel like you are actually travelling the world.

First Light delivers.

The campaign takes Bond across locations including Slovakia, Malta, Iceland, Mauritania and London, giving the adventure the globetrotting structure you would expect from a Bond story.

And the variety matters.

You are constantly moving into a new environment.

New people.

New objectives.

New problems.

New ways to get yourself killed.

It is properly Bond.

The Set Pieces

And then IO Interactive says:

“Oh, you thought we were making a stealth game?”

Nope.

Here's the helicopter.

Here's the car chase.

Here's the explosion.

Here's Bond falling from somewhere he absolutely should not survive.

Here's another explosion.

And here's the suit.

The set pieces are some of the strongest parts of the campaign.

They are cinematic without completely taking control away from you.

That distinction matters.

A game can show you an amazing action sequence.

A great game makes you feel like you caused it.

First Light gets surprisingly close to that.

The Little Moments Matter

Here is where I think First Light deserves more credit: the quiet moments.

Bond learning how to tie his bow tie.

His relationship with Q.

His interactions with M.

Moneypenny.

His awkwardness.

His arrogance.

His gradual understanding of what being a 00 agent actually means.

One of the most memorable moments is literally Q helping Bond with his tie.

There is no giant explosion.

Nobody dies.

But that small interaction tells you more about Bond's transformation than another twenty-minute exposition dump could.

That's good writing.

John Greenway

And I did not expect this...

but John Greenway became one of my favorite characters.

Lennie James absolutely owns the role.

He is Bond's mentor.

His superior.

Sometimes his babysitter.

And occasionally the guy who looks like he is one bad day away from strangling Bond himself.

Their relationship gives the story an emotional backbone.

Greenway is not simply there to explain missions.

His own history feeds into the narrative, and Bond gradually begins seeing him differently.

It is one of the game's strongest relationships.

The Story — Bond Before He Became Bond

The origin-story structure works because Bond actually changes.

He begins as a reckless young agent who thinks he is basically invincible.

Then reality hits.

Hard.

The story is ultimately interested in Bond's development and the consequences of the life he has chosen.

That decision strengthens the narrative because First Light is not simply asking:

“Can Bond stop the villain?”

It is asking:

“What happens when this cocky young man realizes the job he chose can actually destroy people?”

That is much more interesting.

The Supporting Cast

M.

Q.

Moneypenny.

Greenway.

Cressida.

Monroe.

They do not all receive exactly the same amount of development, but the overall cast is strong.

And importantly, they do not feel like cardboard NPCs waiting for Bond to arrive.

Q in particular is fantastic.

He is stylish.

Intelligent.

Funny.

And somehow manages to make Bond look underdressed.

The Villains

Here is where I have to take my first real swing at the game:

The villains aren't as strong as the heroes.

They are functional.

The larger conspiracy works.

The stakes work.

But compared with the personality of Bond, Greenway, Q and M, the antagonistic side sometimes feels less memorable.

That is one area where I think a sequel could improve significantly.

Give Bond a villain who can actually match him personality for personality.

Not just someone who wants to destroy the world because villain.exe has started running.

Presentation

This game is gorgeous.

IO Interactive's Glacier engine is doing serious work.

Characters look fantastic.

Environments are packed with detail.

Clothing looks absurdly good.

Cars look incredible.

And the world feels expensive.

That last part is important.

007 First Light genuinely creates the feeling that you are playing through your own Bond film.

You do not just see Bond.

You feel like you're inside a Bond movie.

The Bond Music

And then there is the music.

A Bond game needs musical identity.

It needs that feeling of:

Oh shit. We're doing Bond now.

The soundtrack knows when to be elegant.

When to be tense.

When to go completely fucking insane.

And when that classic Bond energy kicks in?

Chef's kiss.

The Campaign

You are looking at roughly 14–15 hours for the main campaign, depending on your pace and how much you explore.

And I think that is almost perfect.

It is long enough to feel like an actual Bond adventure, but short enough that it does not become:

“Congratulations. You've been playing this mission for 47 hours.”

There is also replayable TacSim content, giving players more reason to return after finishing the main campaign.

The game understands that a Bond adventure does not need to be enormous.

It needs to be memorable.

Where First Light Falls Short

Now...

The Game Times is not here to suck IO Interactive's dick.

There are problems.

The driving is not always as smooth as it should be.

Some combat encounters can feel longer than necessary.

The game occasionally struggles to balance its cinematic ambitions with its gameplay.

And the villains and broader conspiracy are not always as compelling as the character work.

Those criticisms are fair.

But here is the thing:

None of them break the fantasy.

And that is what matters most.

When the game puts you behind the wheel, hands you a gadget, gives you a gun and throws you into another ridiculous situation, the Bond fantasy remains intact.

Is This the Best James Bond Game Ever?

That is a dangerous sentence.

Because GoldenEye 007 is not just a game.

It is fucking history.

But if we judge them within their respective eras?

First Light absolutely deserves to be in the conversation.

It delivers a modern Bond fantasy with cinematic storytelling, contemporary stealth, gadgets, melee combat, huge set pieces and meaningful character development without losing the soul of Bond.

And that is incredibly difficult.

Ariya's Final Take

THE GAME TIMES REVIEWS

Here is the truth.

I did not need 007 First Light to be perfect.

I needed it to make me feel like James Bond.

And it fucking did.

I walked into missions thinking about how I would approach them.

I started recognizing opportunities.

I started improvising when everything went wrong.

I started caring about the people around Bond.

And eventually...

I started getting that stupid little grin when the game gave me a car, a suit, a gun and a completely unreasonable objective.

That's Bond.

IO Interactive did not make a game about James Bond.

They made a game about becoming James Bond.

And that is the difference.

First Light is not perfect.

The villains could be stronger.

Some combat encounters drag.

The driving needs refinement.

But the core?

The core is fucking special.

After years of waiting for someone to bring Bond back to gaming, IO Interactive did not just bring him back.

They gave him a future.

9.5
THE GAME TIMES SCORE
ONE OF THE BEST JAMES BOND GAMES EVER MADE

One of the best single-player games of 2026.

Resident Evil Requiem still owns my horror heart.

PRAGMATA owns my surprise-of-the-year heart.

But 007 First Light?

That's the one that made me feel fucking cool.

IO Interactive...

You've got my attention.

Now give me Bond 2.

Shaken. Stirred. And absolutely fucking worth playing.
