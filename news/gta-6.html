GTA 6’s Extended Look Is Coming to Netflix First — And Gamers Aren’t Happy About It

Rockstar Games is giving Grand Theft Auto VI its first extended showcase on August 27, but there’s a catch: Netflix subscribers will get to watch it first.

AUGUST 13, 2026 • THE GAME TIMES

Rockstar Games has officially announced Grand Theft Auto VI: An Extended Look, a new showcase offering fans their first extended look at the highly anticipated game.

The presentation will premiere on Netflix on August 27, before becoming available to everyone else later that day through YouTube and Rockstar Games’ official website. Rockstar has also released a short teaser confirming the event.

And yes — before anyone gets too excited, this isn't the game itself.

“No... not the game. Calm down.” — Ariya

Still, this could be a big moment for GTA 6. Rockstar has remained extremely selective about what it reveals, and An Extended Look is expected to provide significantly more footage than the game's previous trailers.

Exactly what Rockstar plans to show, however, remains under wraps.

Netflix Gets There First

Netflix has described the deal as a “first-of-its-kind partnership” with Rockstar.

The arrangement means Netflix subscribers will receive access to GTA VI: An Extended Look first on August 27, while everyone else will be able to watch it for free on YouTube and Rockstar’s website roughly six hours later.

That delay has already sparked criticism among gamers.

The issue isn't necessarily the six-hour wait itself. It's the idea of putting a highly anticipated gaming showcase behind a subscription service — even temporarily.

Netflix gets a major event capable of driving attention and engagement toward its platform. Rockstar gets an unusual marketing partnership surrounding arguably the biggest upcoming game in the industry.

Gamers without Netflix? They wait.

“A first-of-its-kind partnership.” — Netflix

And that's where the reaction gets interesting.

Rockstar already has one of the largest gaming audiences in the world. Whenever the company releases new GTA 6 footage, millions of people are going to watch regardless of where it's hosted.

It doesn't exactly need Netflix to generate hype.

Rockstar Has One Job Now: Show the Game

The biggest question surrounding An Extended Look isn't really Netflix.

It's gameplay.

Rockstar's previous GTA 6 trailers have broken records and generated enormous attention, but fans have now seen enough cinematic footage to know that the game looks incredible.

What they really want is to see how GTA 6 actually plays.

How does the world react to the player? How deep are the NPC systems? How does driving feel? What does combat look like? How much freedom will players have?

After years of waiting, another beautifully edited cinematic trailer probably isn't going to satisfy everyone.

One tiny Rockstar teaser is already enough to send millions of people into meltdown mode.

Very few studios still have that kind of power.

Now Rockstar has to decide whether to use it for another trailer...

or finally let us play the damn thing — visually, at least.

The Netflix partnership may be unusual, but if August 27 delivers a substantial gameplay showcase, gamers probably won't care about the six-hour wait for long.

If it's another cinematic montage?

Well...

Rockstar might have another internet war on its hands.

Fair Marketing Move — or the Beginning of Trailers Becoming Premium Content?

That's ultimately what makes this partnership so interesting.

A six-hour delay isn't going to stop GTA 6 from becoming one of the biggest gaming conversations of the year. But putting the first viewing window behind a paid streaming subscription is certainly an unusual choice.

If Rockstar delivers the gameplay fans have been waiting for, the Netflix controversy will probably become a footnote.

But if An Extended Look turns out to be another cinematic showcase with little meaningful gameplay, expect the internet to have plenty to say.
